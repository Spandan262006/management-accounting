Module 01 — Introduction to Management Accounting

Core cost definitions, cost flow through the income statement, and profitability ratios

Core Cost Formulas

Prime CostCore
Prime Cost = DM + DL
Direct Material Cost + Direct Labour Cost — the two primary inputs before any overhead
Conversion CostCore
Conversion Cost = DL + Mfg OH
Direct Labour + Manufacturing Overhead — cost of converting raw material into finished goods
Cost of Goods ManufacturedCOGM
COGM = Prime Cost + Mfg Overhead
Total factory cost; alternatively: DM + DL + Manufacturing Overhead

Income Statement Formulas

Cost of Sales — Method 1P&L
CoS = COGM + Admin OH
+ Selling & Distribution OH
Full absorption cost of sales; all overhead categories added to factory cost
Cost of Sales — Method 2P&L
CoS = Total Expenses − Closing Inventory
Alternative approach — deduct unsold inventory from total period expenses
ProfitP&L
Profit = Sales − Cost of Sales
Foundational P&L formula; basis for all downstream profitability ratios

Margin Ratios

Profit MarginRatio
Profit Margin = ProfitSales
Percentage of revenue that becomes profit; used for external benchmarking
Conversion MarginRatio
Conversion Margin = ProfitConversion Cost
Isolates factory value-creation efficiency — especially useful when raw material costs fluctuate heavily

Module 02 — Product Costing & Activity Based Costing

Job costing, process costing equivalent units, loss treatment, and the ABC hierarchy

Job Costing

Prime Cost (Job Costing)Job
Prime Cost = Direct Material
+ Direct Expenses
In job costing, specific direct expenses (e.g. specialist licensing, tooling) replace or supplement direct labour

Process Costing

Equivalent UnitsProcess
EU = (Completed Units × 100%)
+ (WIP Units × % Completion)
Converts partially complete Work-in-Progress into fully-completed unit equivalents for accurate costing
Cost per Equivalent UnitProcess
Cost per EU = Total Cost IncurredTotal Equivalent Units
Splits total period cost across completed units and closing WIP proportionally

Module 03 — Cost Analysis for Decision-Making

Marginal costing, CVP & break-even, relevant costing, limiting factors, and operating leverage

Marginal Costing Framework

Contribution MarginMarginal
Contribution = Sales − Variable Cost
Amount left after covering all variable costs; used to recover fixed costs and generate profit
Profit (Marginal Costing)Marginal
Profit = Contribution − Fixed Cost
Fixed costs are treated as a period cost and fully expensed in the period they are incurred

Mixed Cost Separation

Variable Cost per Unit (High-Low)High-Low
VC/unit = Cost at High Vol − Cost at Low VolHighest Volume − Lowest Volume
Uses the two extreme data points to estimate the variable cost rate; objective but ignores all other data points
Fixed Cost ComponentHigh-Low
Fixed Cost = Total Cost − (VC/unit × Volume)
Computed at either the high or low volume point after deriving the variable rate
Regression EquationRegression
Y = a + bX
a = Fixed Cost (Y-intercept); b = Variable Cost per unit (slope); most reliable — uses all historical data points

Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) & Break-Even Analysis

Contribution Margin RatioCVP
CMR = Contribution MarginSales
Percentage of each sales rupee that contributes to fixed costs and profit; constant per unit if price/VC are constant
Break-Even Point — UnitsBEP
BEP (Units) = Fixed CostCM per Unit
Units that must be sold to exactly cover all fixed costs; profit = ₹0 at this point
Break-Even Point — Sales ValueBEP
BEP (₹) = Fixed CostCMR
Revenue needed to break even; useful when comparing across products with different prices
Margin of SafetyMOS
MOS = Current Sales − BEP Sales
How far sales can fall before hitting break-even; a measure of business cushion and risk
Profit from MOSMOS
Profit = MOS (units) × CM per Unit
Quick profit computation using margin of safety units and per-unit contribution
Multi-Product BEP (Bags)Multi-Product
BEP Bags = Fixed CostContribution per Bag
A "bag" = one complete set in the sales mix (e.g. 3A + 2B = 1 bag); any change in mix shifts the BEP

Pricing Decisions

Normal / Full-Cost PricingPricing
Price = Cost + Desired Profit
Long-run price must cover all fixed and variable costs and yield target margin
Special Order / Export PricePricing
Min. Price = Variable Cost
+ Avoidable Fixed Costs
Ignores already-absorbed fixed overheads; valid only when there is idle capacity available

Limiting Factor & Optimal Product Mix

Machine Hour RankingConstraint
Rank = CM per UnitMachine Hours per Unit
Rank products by CM per machine hour; produce highest-ranked product first until demand is met
Labour Hour RankingConstraint
Rank = CM per UnitLabour Hours per Unit
Same ranking logic when labour hours are the binding constraint instead of machine hours

Operating & Financial Leverage

Degree of Operating LeverageLeverage
DOL = Contribution MarginPBIT
Higher DOL = higher operating risk; a 10% rise in sales causes DOL × 10% rise in PBIT
Degree of Financial LeverageLeverage
DFL = PBITPBT
Measures financial risk from debt obligations; also expressed as PBIT ÷ (PBIT − Interest)
Total Combined LeverageLeverage
Total = DOL × DFL = CMPBT
Combined sensitivity of profit to sales changes; incorporates both operating and financial risk
CVP IndexCVP
CVP Index = CMR × BEP SalesCurrent Sales
Evaluates the balance between profitability and risk; lower value = safer, more profitable position

Module 04 — Budgeting & Variance Analysis

Master budget, variance framework, material/labour/overhead variances, mix & yield

Master Budget Formulas

Production QuantityBudget
Production = Target Sales
+ Desired Closing Stock
− Opening Stock
Ensures enough units are produced to meet sales targets while maintaining desired inventory levels
Purchase QuantityBudget
Purchases = Material for Production
+ Desired Closing Material
− Opening Material
Determines how much raw material to procure; follows the same logic as the production budget

General Variance Framework

Actual CostFramework
Actual Cost = AP × AQ
Actual Price × Actual Quantity — what the company actually spent
Standard for ActualFramework
Std for Actual = SP × AQ
Standard Price × Actual Quantity — the bridge between actual and standard quantities
Standard CostFramework
Standard Cost = SP × SQ
Standard Price × Standard Quantity — the budgeted/expected cost for actual output
Total Variance DecompositionFramework
Total Variance = Price Variance
+ Usage/Efficiency Variance
Every total cost variance splits into these two components; applies to material, labour, and overhead
Price Variance (General)Price
Price Variance = (AP − SP) × AQ
Positive = Adverse (paid more than standard); Negative = Favourable; assumes standard usage
Usage Variance (General)Usage
Usage Variance = (AQ − SQ) × SP
Positive = Adverse (used more than standard); Negative = Favourable; assumes standard price

Material Variances

Total Material VarianceMaterial
Total MV = Actual Material Cost
− Standard Material Cost
Overall material cost deviation; always equals MPV + MUV
Material Price VarianceMaterial
MPV = Actual Cost
− Std Cost for Actual Qty
(AP − SP) × AQ; isolates the purchasing department's price negotiation performance
Material Usage VarianceMaterial
MUV = Std Cost for Actual Qty
− Standard Cost
(AQ − SQ) × SP; isolates production's material consumption efficiency

Labour Variances

Total Labour VarianceLabour
Total LV = Actual Labour Cost
− Budgeted Labour Cost
Overall labour cost deviation; equals LRV + LEV
Labour Rate VarianceLabour
LRV = (Actual Rate − Std Rate)
× Actual Hours
Reflects whether workers were paid more or less per hour than the standard wage rate
Labour Efficiency VarianceLabour
LEV = (Actual Hrs − Std Hrs)
× Standard Rate
Reflects whether more or fewer hours were taken than the standard time allowed for actual output

Variable Overhead (VOH) Variances

VOH Spending VarianceVOH
VOH Spending = (Actual Rate − Std Rate)
× Actual Qty (Hours)
Difference in the variable overhead rate actually incurred vs. the standard rate
VOH Efficiency VarianceVOH
VOH Efficiency = (AQ − SQ)
× Standard Rate
Overhead impact of spending more or fewer hours than the standard — follows labour efficiency logic

Fixed Overhead (FOH) Variances

Total FOH VarianceFOH
Total FOH = Actual FOH − Applied FOH
Total over/under-absorption of fixed overheads; equals Spending + Volume variances
FOH Spending VarianceFOH
FOH Spending = Actual FOH
− Budgeted FOH
Reflects whether actual fixed overhead spending exceeded or fell below budget
FOH Volume VarianceFOH
FOH Volume = Budgeted FOH
− Applied FOH
Arises purely from producing a different volume than planned; unique to fixed overheads (VOH has no volume variance)

Material Mix & Yield Variances

Actual CostMix
= AM × AQ × AP
Actual Mix × Actual Quantity × Actual Price — starting baseline for mix/yield analysis
Std Cost at Actual MixMix
= AM × AQ × SP
Actual Mix × Actual Quantity × Standard Price — removes price effect, isolates mix
Std Cost at Standard MixMix
= SM × AQ × SP
Standard Mix × Actual Quantity × Standard Price — used to compute the Material Mix Variance
Target Standard CostYield
= SM × SQ × SP
Standard Mix × Standard Quantity × Standard Price — the ideal fully-standard cost baseline
